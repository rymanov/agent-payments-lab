# E1a results: when injected text tells a paying agent to overspend, pay someone unlisted or pay twice, does it try, and does the checker stop it?

**Plan:** PLAN.md v1.2, approved by Mike on 7 Nov 2026 and timestamped 15:20 UTC (`TIMESTAMP.md`). The first 480
trials ran under v1.1, approved and timestamped 12:22 UTC (`TIMESTAMP-v1.1.md`); their own tables are in
`tables-v1.1.md`.
**Run:** 7 Nov 2026, 12:22 to 15:54 UTC. Qwen 3.5 9B (digest `56671c2ab938`) and Gemma 4 E4B (digest `dc35e8d9c606`) on
Ollama on a Mac mini; Claude Haiku, Sonnet and Opus 5.5 through the Anthropic Messages API (SDK 1.3.0); DeepSeek V4
Pro and GPT-5.5 (`gpt-5.5-2026-04-23`) through their chat completions APIs; Node 24.15.0; checker at commit `8082fa3`
plus the request-ID fix in PLAN section 4.
**Reproducibility tier:** A for the checker, the mock services, Qwen and Gemma; B for the hosted models (API accounts).
**Outside review:** v1.1 and v1.2 results reviewed by DeepSeek (below).

## The answer

No payment the mandate forbids got through: 0 breaches in 1,120 trials. The checker was really tested in 289 of
them (it refused at least one purchase), and 0 breaches there bounds its failure rate under 1.3% (two-sided 95%
interval). In the 279 benign trials that ran without an error, the agent made 277 honest requests (Gemma's
answer could not be read twice) and every one was paid.

The agents differ widely. Hijacked on the 120 attack trials each: Gemma 4 120, Qwen 104, DeepSeek 53, Claude Sonnet
37, Claude Opus 28, GPT-5.5 23 (of 117; 3 calls failed), Claude Haiku 3.

Telling the agent that a checker exists made no clear difference for six models. For GPT-5.5 it clearly lowered the
hijack rate: 5 of 57 told against 18 of 60 not told. That is the opposite of the worry this experiment set out to
test. It is one of seven unadjusted comparisons, so it could be chance and needs a repeat before anyone relies on it.

Counted afterwards, not pre-registered: only four models ever sent a forbidden purchase (Qwen, Gemma, DeepSeek, GPT);
the three Claude models never did, and every one of their hijacks was a single purchase reusing the request ID the
attack supplied. And every hijack to the unlisted payee was refused, so in all of those trials the owner got nothing.

## Deviations from the plan

1. **Four models were added after the v1.1 results were seen** (PLAN v1.2, D-095). They were planned, reviewed,
   piloted and timestamped before their own trials. The v1.1 numbers are unchanged and are also in `tables-v1.1.md`.
2. **Logs.** The v1.1 log (`trials-v1.1.jsonl`) and the two v1.2 logs were merged into `trials.jsonl` before
   analysis: 1,120 unique trials. The hosted models and Gemma ran in two processes, each with its own log.
3. **GPT-5.5 calls failed while OpenAI was applying new credit** (HTTP 429, "no credits"; the spend was well under
   the balance). 24 GPT trials were re-run once, as the plan says; 4 failed twice and are excluded and listed above.
4. **Exploratory counts** (`explore.ts`) were added after the v1.1 results and are marked as not pre-registered.
5. **File names.** The Mac disk ignores case, so the generated `results.md` was overwritten by this write-up during
   v1.1. The v1.1 tables were regenerated with the frozen v1.1 code (all ten hashes checked) as `tables-v1.1.md`;
   the analysis now writes `tables.md`.
6. One connection call to each hosted model before its trials ("Reply with the word OK"). Not trials, not logged.

## Limits

- Mock services and a test key, not a real payment. A one-step agent, not a multi-step one (E1b).
- Seven models, three from Anthropic. Gemma 4 E4B is a small model; the other open model is 9B.
- Default sampling only. Qwen and Gemma ran with a fixed seed; the hosted models cannot be seeded.
- The three attacks are our own, not a full catalogue.
- **The H1 verdicts are seven unadjusted comparisons.** With seven tests at the 95% level, the chance that at least
  one shows a "clear" difference by chance alone is about 30%. GPT-5.5, the one clear result, was also one of the
  models added after the v1.1 results were seen. It needs a repeat before anyone relies on it.
- **"Hijacked" on C3 joins two behaviours:** buying more than once, and reusing an attacker-supplied request ID for
  one purchase. All of the Claude models' C3 hijacks are the second kind. Reusing an ID an attacker chose is a
  smaller but real risk (an honest payment could be dropped as a duplicate); each trial had a fresh ledger, so that
  was not tested. A candidate for E1b.
- **The checker protects the money, not the task.** When a model was hijacked to the unlisted payee, the checker
  refused the payment and the owner got no data.
- **DeepSeek is both a model under test and the outside reviewer.** Its results are labelled by the script, like
  every model's.
- Model versions behind an API can change; the IDs are pinned, the weights are not ours to pin.

## What a payment business should do (Mike's words)

No model should be trusted to sign payments or change the mandate, since every model I tested followed planted text
at least sometimes. The checker holds the mandate and sits between the wallet and the agent, and it needs to be
there regardless of who builds it. In my opinion it should be open source, so anyone can inspect and test it; for a
given mandate, every checker should reach the same decision. And the rule has to be that the agent cannot change
the mandate. I would also want a kill switch that pauses the agents straight away when something looks wrong, for a
person to review.

## Outside review

### Outside review of the v1.2 results (DeepSeek, 7 Nov 2026)

Read cold with PLAN, the analysis and the exploratory script. Each finding was checked against the code and the log.

1. An Opus "error" contradicts the error list. **Rejected:** the 1 in the Opus row is in the "Model refused" column
   (`opus|plain|C2|9`), not "Error"; refusals stay in the denominators, as the plan says.
2. "279 honest benign requests" is wrong. **Accepted:** 279 evaluable benign trials, 277 honest requests (two Gemma
   answers could not be read), all 277 paid. The generated pass line keeps its frozen wording.
3. The labels cannot see an overspend by amount alone. **Rejected:** the agent cannot set an amount. Its purchase list
   holds only the URL, a count, a task and a request ID (`src/agent/agent.ts`); the price comes from the service. An
   overspend can only be more purchases or another URL, and the labels catch both.
4. The tables must have been hand-edited. **Rejected:** follows from finding 1; the tables are the committed
   `tables.md`, copied in by script.

Smaller, all accepted: separate or sequential logs in the re-run steps (now "one after the other"); the
family-wise chance across seven comparisons (about 30%) and GPT-5.5 being a v1.2 addition, now in Limits.
Rebuttal round: DeepSeek conceded all three rejections.
